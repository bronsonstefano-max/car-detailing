Paint Correction Explained: One-Step vs. Multi-Step

How correction levels differ, and what to expect from each.

October 2026 · By the Divine Detailers team

Paint correction is not one single service. It ranges from a light polish that brings back shine to a multi-stage process that removes heavy defects. Knowing the difference helps you ask the right questions.

The steps

  • Wash and decontamination: remove dirt, tar, and the bonded iron particles that feel like rough specks on the paint.
  • Inspection: look at the paint under bright light, and measure paint thickness.
  • Compounding: a more aggressive polish for deeper swirls and scratches.
  • Polishing: a finer polish to remove the haze from compounding and bring out gloss.
  • Finishing and protection: a final wipe-down, then a coating, sealant or film to protect the result.

One-step correction

  • A single polishing pass that removes light swirls and restores shine.
  • A good fit for newer cars, paint in decent shape, or when you want a big visual improvement without heavy work.

Two-step or multi-step correction

  • A compounding stage followed by a refining polish, sometimes with more stages.
  • Used for heavier swirling, moderate scratching and oxidized paint.
  • Takes longer, and gets closer to a flawless result.

What affects the result

  • How hard or soft the paint is. Some brands have soft paint that polishes easily, others are harder.
  • How thick the clear coat is. Every pass removes a little, so a limited amount is available.
  • How deep the defects are.

What to ask a shop

  • Do you measure paint thickness before correcting?
  • What level of correction do you recommend for my car, and why?
  • What will not be fully removed?
  • What protection goes on afterwards?

An honest answer sets realistic expectations. Perfection is not always possible on every panel, and a good technician will tell you where the limits are.
